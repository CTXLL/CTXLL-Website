Lawn Leveling in Austin & Georgetown, TX

Lawn leveling, also called lawn smoothing, fills the dips, ruts and scalp spots in an established lawn with a sand-and-compost mix. Central Texas Lawn Leveling core aerates first, spreads the mix by machine, drags it smooth, and the grass grows up through it. Most Central Texas yards are done in 1 to 3 sessions.

Signs your yard needs leveling

Scalp marks when you mow

High spots get shaved down to dirt while low spots stay long.

Ankle-rolling dips

Kids and pets can't run without tripping on uneven ground.

Puddles after rain

Water sits in low spots instead of soaking in.

New build or new sod

Builders and sod crews often leave seams and ruts behind.

Our lawn leveling process

Spreading machines, leveling rakes and custom-built drags. No hand-shoveled piles.

  1. 01

    Measure & quote

    We look at the lawn size and how deep the low spots are.

  2. 02

    Core aerate

    Pulls plugs so the mix works into the soil, not just on top of it.

  3. 03

    Spread & drag

    Mix is machine-spread, then dragged and raked into the lows.

  4. 04

    Grow through

    Bermuda or Zoysia grows up through it. Repeat if the yard needs it.

After session 1

Most customers can mow at about half their old height.

After session 2

Mow on the lowest setting, usually about 1.25" on a rotary mower.

Going further

With a reel mower, Bermuda and Zoysia can be cut at 1/4" to 1".

Lawn leveling FAQ

What is the best time of year to level a lawn in Texas?

During the active growing season, once soil temperatures have the grass growing, because the grass has to grow up through the mix. Around Austin that's generally mid-March through October.

How many sessions will my yard need?

Usually 1 to 3, depending on how deep the low spots are.

Can you level a lawn with new sod?

Yes. New sod usually comes with soil that's low in organic matter, so we level it with a compost blend to smooth the seams and start building the soil at the same time. The sod just needs to be established first. We've leveled sod as soon as about 45 days after it was laid.

Should I level with sand, compost or a mix?

A mix. Sand gives you the level surface; compost adds organic matter. Compost isn't required, but over the long run it saves on water and fertilizer and helps the lawn through drought. On newer yards and first-time leveling we use 80% sand and 20% compost, usually for the first two levelings. By the third, we can go to straight sand. Compare leveling mixes

Can you level a shaded yard?

No, we don't level shaded yards. Most homes built in the last 10 years or so are fine, depending on how many trees they have. Shaded lawns can still be topdressed.

Do I need to remove the aeration cores?

No. The cores break down on their own. You only need to remove them if you reel mow.

Does lawn leveling qualify for a rebate?

Yes. The LCRA rebate is still running. Homeowners can get up to $1,200, and commercial properties such as city parks, soccer fields and sports fields can get up to $5,000. Eligibility and the application are set by LCRA (see LCRA WaterSmart rebates); we'll walk you through what you need from us, like the invoice and proof of completion.

How much does lawn leveling cost?

Lawn leveling has an $1,800 minimum, and compost topdressing has a $750 minimum. From there, the price depends on the area, the lawn size and how thick the material goes down. Call for a quote on your yard.
